Selected Work

Selected discoveries

Six first-author results that trace the program from clinical heterogeneity to its biological origins.

  • Individualized treatment response in depression

    Molecular Psychiatry · 2023 IF 10.4

    Individualized functional connectivity reveals distinct neural signatures of antidepressant and placebo response.

  • A brain signature of cocaine use disorder that tracks rTMS response

    Nature Mental Health · 2024 IF 9.3

    A discriminative connectivity signature separates cocaine use disorder from controls and links to who benefits from brain stimulation.

  • Dementia subtypes defined by neuropsychiatric symptoms

    JAMA Network Open · 2024 IF 11.7

    Symptom-associated brain connectivity patterns split dementia into subtypes with distinct clinical profiles.

  • Molecular heterogeneity of amyloid-β and tau pathology

    Alzheimer's & Dementia · 2026 IF 12.8

    Machine learning and transcriptomic integration resolve distinct spatial patterns of Alzheimer's pathology and the molecular programs behind them.

  • What makes a superager resilient

    Under third revision · Science Advances IF 13.9

    The functional connectome of superagers carries early markers of both resilience and vulnerability to Alzheimer's disease. Preprint on bioRxiv.

  • The gut shapes how early the brain starts to age

    In press · eBioMedicine · 2026 IF 11.2

    Brain–gut crosstalk is already associated with accelerated brain aging in young and mid-life adults, across independent cohorts.

About

About Alex

I am a computational neuroscientist interested in a deceptively simple question: why are people different?

My path to this question has crossed biology, bioengineering, artificial intelligence, neuroimaging, psychiatry, aging, and multi-omics. Rather than treating these as separate disciplines, I see them as different levels for understanding the same human system.

Some friends call me by my Chinese courtesy given name, Ziqing (子清). I completed my PhD with Prof. Yu Zhang, spent a postdoc year with Prof. Arpana Church at UCLA learning gut-related omics, and returned to Stanford to continue this work.
